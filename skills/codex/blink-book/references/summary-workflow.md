# Summary workflow

## Understand before dividing

Verify access to the complete source and original figures. Use `source_map.py`, `extract_visuals.py` and `build_source_index.py` when extraction is needed; reuse verified extraction from the same file. Retain source-map, source-index and extraction-log in `_work/`.

- State whether internal knowledge of this book is available to guide the design.
- If unavailable or too uncertain, alert the user before proceeding with source-only planning.
- Use available knowledge to propose the argument, core concepts, complete frameworks and key-idea structure.
- Check the proposal against the contents, opening and closing arguments, named models, relevant passages and figures.
- Record what the source confirms, changes or adds. The source determines the final coverage.
- For source-only planning, derive the proposal from those source sections.
- Write explanations and paraphrases from the cited passages, never from memory.

Keep three short source records:

- `source-structure.md`: source items with IDs and a table containing ID, argument, classification (`core`, `supporting`, `context`) and anchors. Identify essential relationships and exact terms.
- `source-evidence.md`: `## S1: ...` entries connecting claims to direct source passages and concrete evidence. Record the limits of that evidence.
- `key-idea-plan.md`: the central argument, the few concepts a reader must retain, and the explanation's progression. Map source items to key ideas as standalone, merged or omitted; explain selection of supporting material. A core model must remain complete.

An independent source reviewer checks essential coverage and a learner editor checks the intended progression before whole-book drafting. This is the first review round. Capture their decisions briefly and correct the design once as a whole.

## Develop the explanation

Before each key idea, reread its direct source and create a short `idea-argument-packs/key-idea-NN.md`: the reader's problem, conclusion, mechanism, example and connection to the next key idea. Write `key-idea-drafts/key-idea-NN-full-draft.md` before editing the learner file. Parallel authors share the approved conceptual hierarchy and boundaries between key ideas.

Teach the relationship between ideas through the example. Do not turn the page into a succession of definitions. Secondary lists should support the explanation; the reader should know which main concept they belong to and why they matter. Preserve source frameworks without giving every practice equal prominence.

The overview is a separate explanation of the book's argument and central models. It introduces them in bold and explains their relationships before directing readers to the key ideas.

## Edit and check

Use sentence-case headings and preserve exact source terms. Avoid abstract shorthand, slogans, authored em dashes and rhetorical “not X but Y” replacements. Concrete comparisons and necessary qualifications remain welcome. Vary the explanation's form to suit the argument.

Read the actual prose in order as a new reader would. Check the central point, paragraph progression, familiar language, sufficient explanation and technical terms. Rebuild an overloaded key idea around its argument. Do not repair density by shortening every sentence.

For each key idea, retain a concise clarity note stating the central conclusion, how the example explains it, and any material revision. Check the overview and transitions across the whole book. Verify images, labels, source anchors and navigation. Run source-work and prose checks before final review.

Use `summary-review.md` for the complete-content round. Preserve verified unchanged work through subsequent repairs.
